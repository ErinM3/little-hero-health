How triage works

Triage means sorting patients so the sickest are seen first. Many US emergency rooms use a 5-level scale called the Emergency Severity Index (ESI). Level 1 is the most urgent.

LevelWhat it meansExample
1Needs life-saving help right now.Not breathing; a serious allergic reaction with trouble breathing.
2High risk. Should not wait. Confused, in severe pain, or vital signs in the danger zone.Chest pain in an adult; a young baby with a fever.
3Stable, but will need two or more resources (like lab tests, X-rays or an IV).Belly pain that needs blood tests and an ultrasound.
4Stable, needs one resource.A wrist that needs an X-ray; a cut that needs stitches.
5Stable, needs no resources beyond a check-up.A mild rash; a note for school.

SBAR handoff

When a nurse hands a patient to the next shift, they use SBAR so nothing gets missed: Situation (what's happening now), Background (age, history, allergies), Assessment (vital signs and how sick they are), Recommendation (what should happen next).
