Synopsis

A group of friends runs a hospital for a day

The Hero Shift is a hybrid role-play game. Friends gather in one room with one tablet or phone. Each friend picks a real hospital job, dresses in that job's outfit, and the app walks the whole squad through a day in the life of a hospital: start of shift, rounds, the big moment, and end of shift.

The screen is the guide. The play happens in real life. A 4-year-old nurse checks her teddy bear's wristband and listens to its heart. A 12-year-old doctor takes a history with real hospital words. A 17-year-old finishes the shift with a real next step, like booking a CPR class. Adults 21 and up connect to real hospital experiences through official volunteer, shadowing and simulation-lab programs.

The same game grows with the player, from teddy-bear clinic to a real career path. That ladder is the product.

  1. Build the squadEach friend adds their name and age.
  2. Choose a heroEveryone picks a hospital job.
  3. Pick today's actionBike fall, flu day, ambulance call… unlocked by age.
  4. Suit upDress in the real outfit, in real life and on screen.
  5. Play the shiftFour parts of the day, a mission per friend in each.
  6. Earn the badgeStars, a hero badge, and a real-world next step for older players.

Safety line for the 21+ tier. "Being in a real hospital" means official routes only: hospital volunteer offices, shadowing days, EMS ride-alongs, and simulation-lab open houses. Only licensed staff give real patient care. The game never asks a player to act as a clinician with real patients.

Levels & badges

Five loops that keep heroes coming back

Built on self-determination theory: games stay fun when players feel capable, have real choices, and play together. Every reward is free, never sold, and never lost.

Seconds: tap and popAct out a step, tap it, and get an instant pop, stars and confetti.
Minutes: missions+25 XP per mission, team challenges, a bonus mini-game, and the patient's mood rising.
One shift: the show"The hospital is open!" opener, part-of-day banners, the Hero Chest, badges and level-ups.
Weeks: growing up7 ranks, a 16-badge Badge Book with hints for locked badges, 12 stickers, and harder bonus games as heroes level up.
Real life: wear itEvery badge has an iron-on patch, every rank a pin. They come in Shift Boxes and the Marketplace.

Market & competitors

How big this is, and who is close

Nobody we found combines all three parts of this idea: in-room group role play, a digital guide, and an age ladder that ends at real hospital careers. Each part exists on its own, and each part has a proven market.

$123.0BGlobal toy sales in 2025, up 8% on 2024 (Circana / The Toy Association).
$45.6BU.S. toy industry size in 2025 (Circana).
$2–4BPretend-play toy segment estimates for 2025. Firms disagree widely; treat as directional.
$2.2–3BHealthcare simulation market for 2025, growing fast. This is the adult and training end of the ladder.

Closest comparables

KidZania: in-person career role playKid-sized cities where children ages 4–14 role-play 100+ careers, hospitals included. Each location draws roughly 500,000 visitors a year. Proves families pay for real-life role play. Limits: fixed venues, no home play, stops at age 14.
Teddy Bear Hospital programs: the 0–5 tier, already provenMedical students treat children's stuffed animals so kids feel less scared of hospitals. Pediatric researchers found participating children reported significantly lower anxiety. A natural partner, not a competitor.
Pepi Hospital and Pepi Doctor: digital pretend playKids play doctor, nurse or patient on a tablet. Parents use it before real doctor visits. Limits: solo, screen-only, no real-world layer.
Toca Boca World: the kids' safety benchmarkClose to 60 million kids play Toca Boca games monthly. Single-player, COPPA compliant, no third-party ads. The standard to match for under-13s.
Maple Hospital (Roblox): online hospital role play at scaleBuilt with input from a licensed nurse. Roles include doctor, nurse, receptionist and janitor. The online-at-scale partner option for older kids.
Teen pipeline programs: the 13–21 tiersHOSA clubs, hospital nursing camps like Cleveland's Future Nurse Academy, and high school health academies. Hospitals fund these because of nurse shortages. A paying customer for the upper tiers.

What this means for The Hero Shift

The open space is the ladder. Toy companies stop at play, KidZania stops at 14, and hospital pipeline programs start at high school. The Hero Shift can sell to families at the bottom (play kits and the app) and to hospitals and schools at the top (career-pipeline programs), with the same characters running through both.
